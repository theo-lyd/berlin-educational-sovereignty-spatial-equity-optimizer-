# Phase 8 Interpretation Notes — Planning Slippage and Temporal Risk

Planning slippage means the plan changed after it was first published. In practical terms, this often appears as:
- later handover years,
- higher projected costs,
- changing capacity commitments,
- or shifts in project status.

For Berlin public-sector education planning, slippage matters because demand does not wait for construction timelines. When handover dates move later, districts can remain under capacity for longer and interim solutions may be needed. When costs rise, fewer projects can be funded within the same budget envelope, which can delay equity goals across districts.

The historical tracking table captures each planning version over time. The slippage analysis compares one version to the previous version for the same project and flags whether dates moved, costs changed, capacity changed, or status changed. This creates an auditable timeline of planning reliability.

How to read temporal risk in this project:
- Repeated date delays indicate delivery risk.
- Cost increases without matching capacity gains indicate efficiency risk.
- Capacity reductions in high-demand districts indicate equity risk.
- Frequent status changes indicate governance or execution instability.

Policy relevance for thesis framing:
- Temporal risk is not only a project-management issue; it is a service-delivery risk.
- Historical slippage metrics help separate one-off changes from persistent planning drift.
- Decision-makers can prioritize interventions on projects or districts where slippage repeatedly undermines expected capacity delivery.
